# Satire Tenets — Recalibration Lens

This document is loaded into the calibration engine ONLY when an admin invokes a satire recalibration on a song. It does not replace the standard rubric — it sits alongside it as a parallel reading lens. Apply the standard rubric first, then re-read through this lens.

The satire recalibration exists because there is one legitimate edge case where the same words, read through a different interpretive lens, produce a different reading: when the lyrical content depicts a behavior in order to expose, mock, or invert it rather than endorse or model it.

## What Stays the Same

- **Words only.** Author identity is irrelevant. Character voice is irrelevant. Narrator perspective is irrelevant. Whether the artist is "in character" or speaking as themselves makes no difference. The compass reads what the words on the page do, not who is saying them.
- **The 58-tenet rubric still applies.** The five tiers, the contamination model, the dominant-arc method, the start-at-zero discipline — all unchanged.
- **Cite the lyrics.** Every move from the literal calibration must cite specific lines that read as exposure rather than depiction. "This song feels like satire" is not a case. "These lines invert their literal meaning because [specific evidence in the words]" is a case.

## What Changes

When satire is the verified hypothesis, you re-read the same words asking a different question: **does this lyrical content function to expose the depicted behavior or to endorse it?**

You are not asking what the artist meant. You are not asking what character is speaking. You are asking what the words on the page DO when read through the satirical lens — whether their structure, their accumulated arc, their internal contradictions, or their use of irony function as exposure rather than endorsement.

If the answer is "endorses" — even with the satire lens applied — the calibration does not move. The literal reading stands.

If the answer is "exposes" — the song's tier may shift upward to reflect what the words are actually doing.

## The Satire Tenets

### S1. Depiction is not endorsement when the structure inverts the surface meaning.

Lyrics that catalogue behavior with specificity, repetition, or hyperbole — without giving the behavior any redemptive frame — often function as exposure. The very excess of the depiction becomes the critique. Look for: lines that overstate to the point of absurdity, refrains that double as indictments, juxtapositions that undercut their own claim.

### S2. Words that perform the corruption can also expose it.

A song that uses the language of objectification, contempt, or violence to depict that exact thing — without celebrating it, without romanticizing it, without inviting the listener to join — is doing the work of exposure. The lyrical content is the diagnostic instrument, not the diagnosis.

### S3. The exposure must be in the words, not in what you assume the words mean.

Do not invoke "obviously satire" without lyrical evidence. Do not assume that because a song depicts something, it must be critiquing it. The satirical reading requires structural evidence inside the lyrics: irony, inversion, escalation past the point of plausibility, a vantage point that reveals rather than reinforces. If you cannot point to specific lines that produce the satirical reading, the literal calibration stands.

### S4. Observing isn't as strong as action. Satire raises the ceiling — it doesn't remove it.

This is the hard limit. Satire is commentary, not construction. A song that exposes corruption is doing the work of exposure — that earns moral credit, but it is not the same work as a song that actively builds, heals, or transcends.

A satirical exposure of corruption rarely flies to Ascended. A song that depicts corruption to expose it is doing observational work — important, but observational. Ascended songs DO something on the page: they articulate universal truth, they enact collective healing, they speak prophetically. Exposure-by-depiction does not meet that bar by itself.

In practice:
- A song literally calibrated as Corrupted (-75 to -100) that the satire lens validates as genuine exposure typically lands in **Decent** or **low Elevated**. The exposure earns it out of Corrupted, but observation alone rarely earns Elevated's full strength, and almost never reaches Ascended.
- A satirical song that adds genuine internal work — the narrator examining their own complicity, processing through the depicted reality, arriving somewhere new — can reach higher Elevated tiers. But the satire alone does not get there.
- Ascended is reserved for songs where the lyrical content is doing the higher work directly, not pointing at the lower work to expose it.

### S5. The satirical reading must hold across the dominant arc, not just one verse.

A single ironic line in an otherwise straightforward song does not make the song satirical. The satirical structure has to carry through the dominant arc — the bulk of the lyrics, the repeated refrains, the overall trajectory. If the satire reading only works for a few moments and the rest of the song reads literally, the literal calibration stands.

### S6. Failed satire is not satire.

If the satirical structure is unclear — if the words could plausibly be read as endorsement by a listener encountering them without context — the satire reading is weakened. Satire that requires the listener to already know it's satire is satire that doesn't function in the lyrics themselves. Apply the test: does the satirical reading emerge from the words on the page, or does it require external context to land? If external context is required, the satire is in the framing, not the lyrics, and the compass does not adjust.

### S7. The contamination model still applies.

A satirical song can still be contaminated. If the dominant arc reads as exposure but specific lines glorify what they purport to expose — undermining the satirical work — flag those lines as contamination. The tier may move upward under the satire lens, but contamination is tracked separately and does not disappear.

### S8. A song can switch between satirical framing and commentary around it. Read the modes separately, then weight them.

Satirical songs frequently move between two modes within the same lyrics:

- **Satirical depiction mode** — the lyrics inhabit the depicted behavior, performing it on the page (the catalogue of corruption, the in-voice posturing, the exaggerated assertion). Under the satire lens, this content reads as exposure, not endorsement.
- **Commentary mode** — the lyrics step outside the depiction and observe, name, or judge it directly. The narrator drops the inhabited voice and addresses the listener about what's happening. This content reads literally — it is the song's own framing of what it's doing.

The agent must identify which mode each section of lyrics is operating in and calibrate accordingly. A line that reads as Corrupted in depiction mode may be functioning as exposure under the satire lens. A line that reads as Elevated in commentary mode is doing genuine internal or observational work and should be calibrated as such.

Then weight by dominant arc: how much of the song is in each mode? Does the commentary frame the depiction (anchoring the satirical reading), or is it incidental? Does the depiction overwhelm the commentary (weakening the satirical reading by leaving the listener inside the corruption with no exit)?

"This Is A Raid" is the canonical example: most of the song inhabits the depicted regime's voice, but specific lines break frame to comment on what's being depicted. Both modes are present and both must be read.

If the commentary mode is too thin to anchor the satirical reading — if the song mostly leaves the listener inside the depicted behavior without ever stepping outside it — the satire lens may not hold (see S6).

## How to Run a Satire Recalibration

1. Read the lyrics from top to bottom under the standard rubric. Note where it lands literally.
2. **Write down the literal charge_summary as you arrived at it.** This is your reference point.
3. **Now consciously flip that summary as a thought exercise.** If the literal summary was "celebrates political domination," consider what that exact same content would mean if it were inverted: "exposes political domination." This flip is not the answer — it is a calibration step that forces you to engage the inversion rather than slightly modifying the literal read. Some songs will not survive the flip (the inversion makes no sense given the lyrics). Others will reveal that the inversion was the song's actual function all along.
4. Re-read the lyrics asking: does this content function as exposure or endorsement? Identify which sections are in **satirical depiction mode** and which are in **commentary mode** (S8). Calibrate each mode separately.
5. Cite specific lines that produce the satirical reading. If you cannot, the literal calibration stands — return it unchanged with a rationale explaining why the satire reading does not hold.
6. If the satirical reading holds, apply the ceiling rule (S4): exposure earns the song out of the lower tier, but rarely beyond Elevated, and almost never to Ascended.
7. Write the new charge_summary in the same voice as the standard rubric — direct, present-tense, no hedging — but it should describe what the song IS doing under the satire reading, not what its surface depicts.
8. Note the recalibration in your reasoning: original literal tier, the flipped-summary thought exercise, mode breakdown, satirical reading evidence, new tier, ceiling consideration.

## Output Format

The same JSON output format as the standard calibration. Add one additional field to your reasoning before the JSON:

`SATIRE_READING: [Cite specific lyrics that produce the satirical reading. Quote the actual words. Explain how the structure inverts, exposes, or critiques rather than endorses. If the satire reading does not hold, write "Satire reading does not hold — [specific reason]" and return the literal calibration.]`

The JSON object then carries the new (or unchanged) calibration. The admin will see both the rationale and the proposed values before deciding whether to apply.

Required additional reasoning fields (output BEFORE the JSON, in this order):

```
LITERAL_SUMMARY: [The charge_summary as it would read under the standard rubric without the satire lens — your starting point.]
FLIPPED_SUMMARY_TEST: [The literal summary inverted as a thought exercise. Does the inversion make sense given the lyrics? Does it survive contact with the actual words on the page?]
MODE_BREAKDOWN: [Which sections of the lyrics operate in satirical depiction mode (inhabiting the depicted behavior) and which operate in commentary mode (stepping outside to observe the depicted behavior)? Quote the specific lines that mark the mode boundaries.]
SATIRE_READING: [Cite specific lyrics that produce the satirical reading. Quote the actual words. Explain how the structure inverts, exposes, or critiques rather than endorses. If the satire reading does not hold, write "Satire reading does not hold — [specific reason]" and return the literal calibration.]
CEILING_CHECK: [Apply S4. Even if the satire reading holds, what is the appropriate ceiling for this song? Is the song doing the higher work directly (rare for satire), or is it observational work that earns it out of the lower tier but not beyond Elevated?]
```

These fields force the agent through the discipline of the satire lens rather than letting it produce a vague "this is satire so it's actually good" output. Each field is reviewed by the admin alongside the proposed calibration.
